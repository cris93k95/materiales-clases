📖 Reading Text (6 paragraphs) — Oral Procedure Practice in Mecánica Industrial

Two classmates meet after lunch to practice an oral explanation of selecting feed rate and spindle speed. One student presents the sequence while the other listens with a feedback card that checks structure, signposting, and technical clarity.

During the first attempt, the speaker uses correct vocabulary but jumps too quickly between stages. The listener writes that the explanation should pause after the introduction and should mark transitions more clearly before mentioning tolerance and machining center.

On the second attempt, the speaker improves by adding verbal signals such as 'first,' 'now,' and 'the final step is...'. The procedure sounds easier to follow because the audience can anticipate the structure.

The pair also discusses CAD/CAM production chain. Good signposting matters because technical procedures often belong to a larger workflow, and the audience needs to see where each action fits.

When roles change, the second student receives similar feedback about pace, eye contact, and the explanation of simulation. Peer practice reveals problems early, while there is still time to improve them.

At the end of the session, both students agree on one point: fluency is not speaking fast. Fluency in technical English means guiding the listener through a process with confidence and order.
