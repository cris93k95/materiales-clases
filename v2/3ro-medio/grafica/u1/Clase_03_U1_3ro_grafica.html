Objetivo de la clase: Aplicar inglés técnico para explicar normas de seguridad y responsabilidad profesional en Gráfica.

📖 Reading Text (6 paragraphs) — Safety and Professional Responsibility in Gráfica

Camila is a 3rd year student in Gráfica. This unit is called 'My Technical Skills' because students are preparing an individual video presentation in English. In the video, each student must describe three tools or pieces of equipment, explain two systems or processes, and justify why graphic production is important for society.

The class focus today is 'Safety and Professional Responsibility'. In the graphic production lab, technical English is not decorative; it is a practical tool for understanding manuals, safety labels, diagrams, software menus, and international vocabulary. Students use English to name objects accurately and to explain what they can do with them.

For this specialty, the first technical references are Fiery Command WorkStation, print queue, RIP, color profile. These words appear in workshop routines, teacher explanations, catalogues, short manuals, and safety instructions. When students pronounce them correctly, they sound more professional and can participate in technical conversations with more confidence.

The main processes connected to this lesson include checking bleed and crop marks and producing a proof for client approval. Students do not need to master every advanced detail yet, but they do need to explain the basic purpose, the main steps, and the safety conditions of each process in simple English.

This content also connects with the year focus for Gráfica: Fiery Command WorkStation, print workflow, color accuracy. These topics will return during the year in reading tasks, technical vocabulary work, oral activities, and written comprehension. The objective is to build a bridge between English class and real TP learning, not to study isolated words.

By the end of the lesson, students should have evidence for their final video. They should be able to say what they know, what they can do, what they still need to practice, and why their specialty matters. In social terms, schools, companies, public campaigns, and community projects depend on clear printed and digital communication. That is why technical skills are also civic skills.
