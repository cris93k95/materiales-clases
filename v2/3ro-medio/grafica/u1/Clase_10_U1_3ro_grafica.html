📖 Reading Text (6 paragraphs) — Pronunciation and Rehearsal in Gráfica

Camila is a 3rd year student in Gráfica. The lesson 'Pronunciation and Rehearsal' belongs to Unit 1, 'My Technical Skills', a unit focused on technical identity, tools, systems, and social importance. The reading is written as an adapted technical workplace text, similar to the kind of explanation students may find in a manual, training note, or workshop guide.

In the graphic production lab, English is used to identify tools, follow procedures, read labels, and explain decisions. This is especially important for graphic production because many manuals, digital interfaces, catalogues, and safety warnings use international English terms. The language objective today is connected to Pronunciation focus + word stress.

The technical vocabulary for this class includes tools and concepts such as Fiery Command WorkStation, print queue, RIP, color profile. These terms are not decorative words for a list; they are words that help students describe real work. When a student says the word accurately, writes it correctly, and understands its purpose, the student is closer to professional communication.

The first process connected to this lesson is checking bleed and crop marks. The second process is producing a proof for client approval. Students are not expected to master every advanced detail yet, but they should understand the purpose of the process, identify the main steps, and explain at least one risk or quality requirement in simple English.

This lesson also connects with Fiery Command WorkStation. A system has parts, inputs, outputs, rules, and possible failures. If students learn to describe those elements in English, they can answer reading questions, prepare subtitles, write short reports, and speak about their technical skills with more confidence.

The final purpose is not only to pass an English activity. By the end of the lesson, students should collect ideas for an oral or written product connected to the unit. In social terms, schools, companies, public campaigns, local businesses, and community projects depend on clear printed and digital communication. For that reason, technical English supports employability, safety, service, and civic responsibility.
