📖 Reading Text (6 paragraphs) — Oral Explanation of a Case in Mecánica Automotriz

Before speaking in front of the group, a trainee reviews a real case from the automotive workshop. The case began during inspecting fixed and moving engine parts, continued through a short diagnosis, and ended with a clear recommendation based on evidence.

The trainee organizes the explanation into four parts: the symptom, the evidence, the probable cause, and the lesson learned. To support the explanation, the student highlights key terms such as cooling system, fault code, and scanner.

The goal is not to memorize a perfect speech. The goal is to explain the case clearly enough that a classmate can understand what happened and why one decision was better than another.

The instructor reminds the group that active and passive safety systems should appear only when it helps clarify the case. A short explanation with a clear structure is stronger than a long explanation full of disconnected details.

Students rehearse with past forms and cause-and-effect connectors. They also practice short transitions such as first, then, because of this, and finally. Those expressions guide the listener through the logic of the case.

At the end of the rehearsal, the trainee sounds more confident. Technical English becomes useful when it supports a clear explanation, not when it overwhelms the audience with isolated vocabulary.
