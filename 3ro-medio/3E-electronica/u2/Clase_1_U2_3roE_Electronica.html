🎯 Identificar el problema de la basura electrónica (lectura A2) 📘 OA3 🗂 Clase 1 — Unidad 2 (Clase 10 del año) 🗓 Semana 13 · mayo

Objetivo de la clase

Identificar el problema de la basura electrónica (e-waste) y su impacto ambiental, a partir de un texto introductorio en inglés nivel A2, y compartir opiniones simples.

🟢 Inicio (15 min)

🔎 Brainstorm + vocabulario

Brainstorm: "What do you do with your old phone, broken charger or old TV?" (5 min).

Vocabulario nuevo:

e-waste (i-ueist) — basura electrónica
landfill (LAND-fil) — vertedero
recycling (ri-SAI-kling) — reciclaje
toxic (TOK-sik) — tóxico
circuit board (SUR-kit bord) — placa de circuito
repair (ri-PER) — reparar
🟪 Desarrollo (60 min)

📖 Actividad 1 — Reading introduction (20 min)

Texto adaptado nivel A2.

1. Every year, the world produces more than 60 million tons of e-waste: old phones, computers, TVs, chargers, batteries and small home appliances. This is the fastest-growing type of waste in the world. By 2030, the number will reach 75 million tons. In Chile, every person produces around 9 kg of e-waste per year — that's more than 170,000 tons in total.

2. Why is e-waste a problem? Inside a phone or a computer, there are toxic materials: lead, mercury, cadmium and brominated flame retardants. When old electronics go to a normal landfill, these materials can contaminate the soil and the water. They can also reach the food chain and damage human health, especially for children.

3. The good news is that 80% of an electronic device can be recycled. The metals on a circuit board — gold, silver, copper, palladium — have real value. One ton of mobile phones contains more gold than one ton of gold ore from a mine. In Chile, only 3.4% of e-waste is recycled today, but in 2024 a new law (Ley REP) obliges companies that sell electronics to take back and recycle them.

4. What can YOU do as a future electronics technician? First, learn to repair devices instead of throwing them away. A phone with a broken screen can have a new life. Second, learn to recover useful components from old boards. Third, study the international standards: WEEE Directive, RoHS, ISO 14001. All these standards are in English. The "right to repair" movement is growing in Europe and the USA, and Chile will follow soon. Technicians who know repair, recycling AND English will have the best jobs.

📝 Actividad 2 — Comprehension (15 min): 6 preguntas

1. How much e-waste does each Chilean produce per year?

Around 9 kg per person per year (more than 170,000 tons total).

2. Name 3 toxic materials found in electronics.

Lead, mercury, cadmium, brominated flame retardants.

3. What percentage of e-waste is recycled in Chile today?

Only 3.4%.

4. (Opinión) Why do you think people throw away their phones so quickly?

Respuesta personal: moda, obsolescencia programada, precios bajos, falta de información, etc.

5. (Opinión) Would you buy a refurbished (reacondicionado) phone? Why?

Respuesta personal.

6. (Opinión) What's the most important action YOU can take to reduce e-waste?

Respuesta personal: reparar, donar, reciclar correctamente, comprar menos, etc.

✍️ Actividad 3 — Vocabulary in context (10 min)

Escribe 1 oración con cada palabra del vocabulario. Ejemplo: "My old phone is e-waste."

🗣 Actividad 4 — Pair discussion (15 min)

Discute preguntas 4, 5 y 6 en parejas usando:

Sentence starters:

"I think… because…" · "In my opinion…" · "I agree because…" · "I disagree because…"

Formativa: 6 preguntas + 3 palabras en oraciones + 1 sentence starter.
