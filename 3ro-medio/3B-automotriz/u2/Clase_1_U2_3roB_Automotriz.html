🎯 Identificar el impacto ambiental de los vehículos (lectura A2) 📘 OA3 🗂 Clase 1 — Unidad 2 (Clase 10 del año) 🗓 Semana 13 · mayo

Objetivo de la clase

Identificar el impacto ambiental de los vehículos a combustión y el cambio hacia autos eléctricos, a partir de un texto introductorio en inglés nivel A2, y compartir opiniones simples con sentence starters.

🟢 Inicio (15 min)

🔎 Brainstorm + vocabulario

Brainstorm: "What global problems are connected to cars?" (5 min).

Vocabulario nuevo:

pollution (po-LU-shon) — contaminación
emissions (e-MI-shons) — emisiones
fossil fuel (FO-sil FIU-el) — combustible fósil
electric vehicle (EV) (e-LEK-trik VI-i-kul) — vehículo eléctrico
battery (BA-te-ri) — batería
hybrid (JAI-brid) — híbrido
🟥 Desarrollo (60 min)

📖 Actividad 1 — Reading introduction (20 min)

Texto adaptado nivel A2.

1. Today there are more than 1.4 billion cars in the world. Most of them use gasoline or diesel — fossil fuels. When the engine burns these fuels, it produces emissions: CO₂, nitrogen oxides and small particles. These emissions are the main cause of air pollution in big cities like Santiago, Beijing and Mexico City.

2. Cars are also a big cause of climate change. Transportation produces about 24% of global CO₂ emissions. In Chile, a normal car produces around 2.5 tons of CO₂ every year. That is the same as cutting down 100 small trees.

3. The good news is that the industry is changing. Electric vehicles (EVs) have a battery instead of a fuel tank. They produce zero direct emissions. Tesla, BYD, Nissan and Volvo make many EV models. In Chile, there are now more than 5,000 electric cars on the road, and Santiago has more than 800 electric buses — one of the largest fleets in Latin America. Hybrid cars use both gasoline and electricity and are a popular first step.

4. But electric cars also have problems. The batteries need lithium, cobalt and other minerals. Mining these minerals can damage the environment, and recycling old batteries is expensive. Also, EVs are more expensive than normal cars, and there are not enough charging stations in Chile yet. Automotive technicians of the future must know how to work with both types of cars — and the technical manuals are mostly in English.

📝 Actividad 2 — Comprehension (15 min): 6 preguntas

1. How much CO₂ does a normal car in Chile produce every year?

Around 2.5 tons per year (same as cutting 100 small trees).

2. What 3 things does a car engine produce when it burns fossil fuel?

CO₂, nitrogen oxides and small particles.

3. How many electric buses are there in Santiago?

More than 800 (one of the largest fleets in Latin America).

4. (Opinión) Do you think Chile is ready for electric cars? Why?

Respuesta personal con razón.

5. (Opinión) Would you buy an electric car? Why or why not?

Respuesta personal.

6. (Opinión) Are EVs really "clean"? Give 1 reason yes and 1 reason no.

Sí: zero direct emissions. No: minería de litio/cobalto, reciclaje caro, energía eléctrica puede venir de fuentes contaminantes.

✍️ Actividad 3 — Vocabulary in context (10 min)

Escribe 1 oración propia con cada una de las 6 palabras del vocabulario. Ejemplo: "My family has a hybrid car."

🗣 Actividad 4 — Pair discussion (15 min)

Comparte tu opinión sobre las preguntas 4, 5 y 6 con un compañero usando:

Sentence starters:

"I think… because…" · "In my opinion…" · "I agree because…" · "I disagree because…"

📋 Seguimiento

Formativa: 6 preguntas completas + 3 palabras del vocabulario en oraciones propias + 1 sentence starter en discusión.

🟣 Cierre (15 min)

🗣 Sharing + expresiones de opinión

3 estudiantes comparten. El docente introduce: "I believe that…" · "According to the text…" · "On the other hand…"

Preview: "Next class we will analyze a real case: The Electric Vehicle Revolution — hybrid vs. electric vs. hydrogen."

🎫 Exit ticket

"I think electric cars are __________ (good / bad / both) because __________."
