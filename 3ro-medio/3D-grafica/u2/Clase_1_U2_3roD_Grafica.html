3°D — Gráfica · Reading introduction: The Environmental Impact of Printing

🎯 Identificar impacto ambiental de la industria gráfica (lectura A2) 📘 OA3 🗂 Clase 1 — Unidad 2 (Clase 10 del año) 🗓 Semana 13 · mayo

Objetivo de la clase

Identificar el impacto ambiental de la industria gráfica y las alternativas sustentables, a partir de un texto introductorio en inglés nivel A2, y compartir opiniones simples.

🟢 Inicio (15 min)

🔎 Brainstorm + vocabulario

Brainstorm: "How much paper does your school use every day? Where does it come from?" (5 min).

Vocabulario nuevo:

printing (PRIN-ting) — impresión
ink (ink) — tinta
recycled paper (ri-SAI-kuld PEI-per) — papel reciclado
chemicals (KE-mi-kals) — químicos
sustainable (sas-TEI-na-bul) — sustentable
waste (ueist) — residuo
🟪 Desarrollo (60 min)

📖 Actividad 1 — Reading introduction (20 min)

Texto adaptado nivel A2.

1. Books, magazines, packaging, posters, T-shirts: printing is everywhere in our lives. But the printing industry has a big environmental cost. Every year, the world produces more than 400 million tons of paper. To make this paper, the industry cuts down 4 billion trees and uses huge amounts of water and energy.

2. The traditional inks used in offset printing contain petroleum and heavy metals. When old printed materials go to the landfill, the inks can release toxic chemicals into the soil. The cleaning solvents used in print shops also produce VOC gases (volatile organic compounds) that pollute the air and are dangerous for workers' health.

3. The good news is that the industry is changing. Many printers now use soy-based inks, which come from soy plants and are much safer. They use recycled paper made from old newspapers and office paper. In Chile, companies like Quad/Graphics and Gráfica Andes are FSC certified — that means their paper comes from sustainable forests. Digital printing is also more sustainable because it produces less waste.

4. The future is "green printing". New graphic designers must know how to choose sustainable materials, how to design with less ink, and how to read certifications in English (FSC, PEFC, ISO 14001). They must also propose digital alternatives — for example, sending an invitation by email instead of printing 200 paper invitations. Being a graphic designer in 2030 means being an environmental designer too.

📝 Actividad 2 — Comprehension (15 min): 6 preguntas

1. How many trees does the world cut down every year for paper?

4 billion trees.

2. What are 2 sustainable alternatives mentioned in the text?

Soy-based inks, recycled paper, FSC-certified paper, digital printing.

3. Name 2 Chilean companies that are FSC certified.

Quad/Graphics and Gráfica Andes.

4. (Opinión) Do you think clients in Chile will pay more for sustainable printing? Why?

Respuesta personal con razón.

5. (Opinión) Would you prefer to design for print or for digital? Why?

Respuesta personal.

6. (Opinión) What is one easy thing YOU can do in your designs to be more sustainable?

Respuesta personal: usar menos tinta, diseños en B/N, recomendar papel reciclado, etc.

✍️ Actividad 3 — Vocabulary in context (10 min)

Escribe 1 oración con cada palabra. Ejemplo: "My school uses recycled paper for posters."

🗣 Actividad 4 — Pair discussion (15 min)

Discute preguntas 4, 5 y 6 en parejas usando:

Sentence starters:

"I think… because…" · "In my opinion…" · "I agree because…" · "I disagree because…"

📋 Seguimiento

Formativa: 6 preguntas + 3 palabras en oraciones + 1 sentence starter.

🟣 Cierre (15 min)

🗣 Sharing + expresiones

3 estudiantes comparten. El docente introduce: "I believe that…" · "According to the text…" · "On the other hand…"

Preview: "Next class — a real case: Green Printing — soy inks, recycled paper, digital alternatives."

🎫 Exit ticket

"I think sustainable printing is __________ because __________."
