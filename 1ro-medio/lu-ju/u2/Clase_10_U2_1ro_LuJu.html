🧠 Actividad 2 — Language Focus

Use these examples before reading. The class text is written at A2 level and the structures below will help you understand, discuss, and write about the topic with more precision.

Passive Voice in technical English

Use passive voice when the process or result is more important than the person who performs the action.

Examples
  • The practice project is checked before the main task begins.
  • The tool kit is used during the second stage of the procedure.
  • The final result is recorded after the inspection.
Sentence frames
  • The ___ is prepared before ...
  • The ___ is tested so that ...
  • At the end, the ___ is reported.
Relative clauses for precise definitions

Use who, which, or that to add essential information about a person, tool, or process.

Examples
  • A technician is a person who checks the practice project carefully.
  • The tool kit is a tool that helps the team collect evidence.
  • A report which includes measurements is easier to verify.
Sentence frames
  • A ___ is a tool that ...
  • A technician is someone who ...
  • The report which ... shows that ...

📖 Actividad 4 — Reading Model — Ensayar con apoyo y retroalimentación

Modelo guiado A2 para la etapa 4/5 del trabajo oral de cierre semestral: Practicar con apoyo visual y mejorar claridad, pronunciación y seguridad.

Before the final delivery, the student rehearses the explanation with notes, a diagram, or a small visual support.

A partner listens with a checklist: Was the sequence clear? Was the pronunciation understandable? Did the speaker include the safety point and the final result?

The speaker then adjusts the outline, removes unnecessary words, and practises difficult technical vocabulary one more time.

Short rehearsals help the student control time and speak more confidently when mentioning details related to tool kit or checklist.

By the end of rehearsal day, the explanation should be clear enough to present in two or three minutes without reading a full script.

📝 Actividad 5 — Reading comprehension

Answer the questions by returning to the text. Focus on evidence, sequence, language use, and professional meaning before checking the answer key.

1. What task or problem is described in the text?

The text presents the rehearsal stage before the final oral delivery.

2. What happens before the main procedure starts?

Before the presentation, the student practises with notes, a partner, and a feedback checklist.

3. Which tools, parts, or notes are important in the reading?

Important details include timing, pronunciation, visual support, and the safety point inside the explanation.

4. How does the text show the main language focus of the class?

The language focus is on clarity and confidence, because good ideas still need understandable delivery.

5. What result, decision, or solution appears at the end?

At the end, the student revises the outline and prepares a stronger final version.

6. What can you infer about good technical work from the text?

Practice helps technical speaking become clearer, shorter, and more confident.
