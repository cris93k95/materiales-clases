📖 Actividad 4 — Reading: The Offset Printing Process Step by Step

Lee 2 veces. Primera lectura (skimming, 3 min): identifica cuántas etapas hay. Segunda lectura (scanning, 7 min): busca información específica para cada etapa.

The Offset Printing Process Step by Step

Offset printing is one of the most widely used techniques in the graphic arts industry. It is called "offset" because the ink is not transferred directly from the plate to the paper, but to an intermediate rubber surface first. This method gives excellent image quality and is ideal for medium and large print runs.

1. File preparation. The job starts in a computer. The design is created in a graphic software and is checked for fonts, images and bleed. The colors are separated into four basic inks: cyan, magenta, yellow and black (CMYK).

2. Plate making. A thin aluminum plate is exposed to a laser, which records the image. One plate is made for each color, so a full-color job requires four plates.

3. Inking and water. On the press, ink and a special fountain solution (water with chemicals) are applied to the plate. The image area accepts ink and rejects water; the non-image area does the opposite.

4. Image transfer. The plate transfers the image to a rubber blanket cylinder. Then the blanket is pressed against the paper by the impression cylinder. This is the moment when the print is made.

5. Color registration. Each of the four colors is printed one after the other. The papers must pass through the machine in perfect alignment, or the colors will not register correctly.

6. Drying. The ink is dried using heat, ultraviolet light or air, depending on the type of ink and paper used.

7. Finishing. Finally, the sheets are trimmed, folded and sometimes bound. The product is inspected and packed for delivery.

The whole process can take a few hours for a small job and several days for a large one, but the quality and consistency are why offset is still the standard for books, magazines, posters and packaging.

📝 Actividad 6 — Reading comprehension

1. ¿Por qué este proceso se llama offset?

Porque la tinta no pasa directo de la plancha al papel: primero se transfiere a un cilindro de caucho intermedio ("the ink is not transferred directly from the plate to the paper, but to an intermediate rubber surface first.").

2. ¿Cuáles son los 4 colores básicos del sistema CMYK?

Cyan, magenta, yellow y black.

3. ¿Cuántas planchas necesita un trabajo a todo color y por qué?

Cuatro planchas, una por cada color (CMYK).

4. ¿Qué hace la fountain solution?

Es una mezcla de agua con químicos que se aplica a la plancha. El área de la imagen acepta tinta y rechaza agua; el área sin imagen acepta agua y rechaza tinta.

5. ¿Qué pasa si la color registration falla?

Los colores no quedan alineados; la imagen sale "movida" o con bordes de color desplazados.

6. ¿Por qué offset sigue siendo el estándar para libros, revistas y packaging?

Por su calidad de imagen y consistencia, especialmente en tiradas medianas y grandes ("the quality and consistency are why offset is still the standard…").

⚖️ Actividad 7 — Comparative discussion: offset vs. digital

En grupos de 3-4, discutan y escriban 3 diferencias usando conectores de contraste.

Conectores útiles
  • however — sin embargo (inicio de oración).
  • on the other hand — por otro lado.
  • although / even though — aunque (al inicio o al medio).
  • while — mientras que.
Ideas para comparar
  • Cantidad de copias (large vs. small runs).
  • Tiempo de preparación.
  • Costo por copia.
  • Calidad de color.
Ejemplo modelo
  • "Offset is ideal for large runs. On the other hand, digital is better for short jobs."
  • "Although offset takes longer to prepare, its quality is superior."
