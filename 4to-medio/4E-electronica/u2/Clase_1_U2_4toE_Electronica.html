📖 Actividad 4 — Reading (25 min): E-Waste: A Global Crisis

Texto adaptado nivel B1. Primera lectura: scanning de ideas principales (5 min). Segunda lectura: con la tabla de análisis al lado.

1. Every year, the world produces more than 60 million tons of e-waste: old phones, broken laptops, dead chargers, obsolete TVs, broken printers and discarded appliances. According to the United Nations, in 2022 the global figure reached 62 million tons — and only 22% of that was recycled in a formal, safe way. The rest ended up in landfills, in informal dumps or, in many cases, was shipped to countries with weak environmental rules.

2. Chile is one of the largest producers of e-waste in Latin America: around 10 kg per person per year. The country has a new Ley REP (Extended Producer Responsibility) that obliges manufacturers and importers to take back electronic products at the end of their life, but the infrastructure for collection and proper recycling is still under construction. Less than 5% of Chilean e-waste is currently processed correctly.

3. The impact goes far beyond the visible problem. Electronic devices contain valuable materials — gold, silver, copper, rare earth metals — and also toxic substances: lead, mercury, cadmium and brominated flame retardants. When a device is burned or buried, these toxins pollute soil, water and air, and seriously harm the people who work in informal dumps, often without protection.

4. For electronics technicians, this is one of the strongest opportunities of the next decade. The world needs people who can diagnose a broken device and decide: can it be repaired? can it be refurbished and sold again? which components can be recovered? what must be safely disposed? In Chile, repair shops, recycling companies and refurbishing centers (e.g. Recylink, Recycla, Triciclos) are actively hiring technicians who understand circuit boards, soldering, microcontroller programming — and who can read service manuals in English.

5. The challenge is that modern devices are designed to be replaced, not repaired. Phones are glued, batteries are non-removable, software updates are blocked after a few years. Many manufacturers prefer the consumer to buy a new device rather than fix the old one. However, a global movement called "right to repair" is pushing back: the European Union has passed laws that force companies to provide spare parts and repair manuals; the United States and Latin American countries are following.

6. The transition to a circular economy — where products are designed to last, be repaired and be recycled — is one of the great sustainability projects of this century. Electronics technicians are at the center of it. The question for our specialty is no longer "How do I fix one device?", but "How can I help my community move from disposable electronics to repairable, recyclable, durable electronics?"

📝 Actividad 6 — Comprehension & analysis (6 preguntas)

1. (Explícita) ¿Cuántas toneladas de e-waste se generaron en el mundo en 2022 y qué porcentaje se recicló formalmente?

62 millones de toneladas. Solo el 22% se recicló formalmente.

2. (Explícita) ¿Qué obliga la Ley REP en Chile?

Obliga a fabricantes e importadores a hacerse cargo de los productos electrónicos al final de su vida útil (recolección y reciclaje).

3. (Explícita) Menciona 2 materiales valiosos y 2 tóxicos que contienen los dispositivos electrónicos.

Valiosos: oro, plata, cobre, tierras raras. Tóxicos: plomo, mercurio, cadmio, retardantes de llama bromados.

4. (Inferencia) ¿Por qué el texto dice que los dispositivos modernos están "diseñados para ser reemplazados, no reparados"?

Porque vienen pegados (no atornillados), las baterías no se pueden quitar, las actualizaciones de software se bloquean después de unos años, y las marcas prefieren que el consumidor compre un equipo nuevo en vez de repararlo.

5. (Análisis) ¿What does this mean for YOUR career? Escribe 2 acciones concretas que tú podrías tomar este año.

Respuesta personal. Ejemplos válidos: hacer práctica en Recylink/Triciclos/Recycla, especializarse en reparación de móviles o placas, aprender soldadura SMD, mejorar lectura de datasheets en inglés, certificarse en manejo de residuos peligrosos.

6. (Crítica) El texto cierra: "How can I help my community move from disposable electronics to repairable, recyclable, durable electronics?" Da una respuesta concreta.

Respuesta personal y crítica. Debe incluir una acción concreta (ej. abrir taller de reparación local, hacer talleres comunitarios de reparación, postular a una empresa de reciclaje, difundir Ley REP) + razón.

🗣 Actividad 7 — Critical discussion en grupos (20 min)

En grupos de 3-4 estudiantes, debatan:

"How will the e-waste crisis affect YOUR future job? Is this a threat or an opportunity?"

  1. Cada miembro comparte su postura inicial (1 min).
  2. El grupo construye una postura común con 2-3 argumentos apoyados en el texto.
  3. Anotan: "Our group believes the e-waste crisis is mainly a __________ because __________, __________ and __________."
  4. Un representante por grupo comparte la postura (1 min) en el cierre.

Lenguaje útil: "We believe…", "Our main argument is…", "According to the text…", "On the other hand…", "In conclusion…".

📋 Seguimiento de la clase

Formativa: el docente recoge la tabla de análisis y observa el debate. Criterios: (1) tabla completa con información del texto, (2) postura grupal con al menos 2 argumentos basados en el texto, (3) uso de al menos 4 palabras del vocabulario, (4) participación activa.
