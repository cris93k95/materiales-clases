📖 Ítem II — Reading comprehension (20 pts)

II.1 — Read the text

An electric motor turns electrical energy into rotation. Inside the motor we find two main parts: the rotor, which rotates, and the stator, which is fixed.

Every motor has a copper winding. When current passes through the winding, it becomes an electromagnet and creates a magnetic field. The interaction with the stator field produces the torque that turns the shaft.

On the nameplate, the electrician reads key values: voltage, current, power, speed and frequency. For example, 380 V, 7 A, 3 kW, 1450 rpm, 50 Hz. If the motor housing becomes very hot, the winding may be damaged.
