📖 Actividad 1 — Reading the case (20 min): When Diagnostics Fail

Texto adaptado nivel B1. Lectura individual silenciosa, luego segunda lectura completando el organizador.

1. Automotores del Sur is a multi-brand dealership in Concepción with a service department of 18 technicians. In the last six months, the customer service manager noticed a worrying pattern: nine cars had been returned to the workshop two, three or even four times for the same complaint — a recurring engine misfire. Each repeated visit cost the dealership reputation, free labor and, in three cases, full warranty claims.

2. The service supervisor, Don Rodrigo Cárdenas (28 years of experience), was asked to investigate. He took the service orders of the nine repeated cases and noticed something all of them had in common: every technician had connected the diagnostic scanner to the OBD-II port, read the fault code (P0301: cylinder 1 misfire detected), and replaced the spark plug and the ignition coil. The fault code came back two weeks later — every time.

3. Don Rodrigo asked one of the customers to leave the car for a full day. He started with a basic visual inspection, something that almost no technician had done. After 20 minutes, he found a partially burnt connector on the wiring harness near the cylinder 1 ignition coil. The connector heated up, lost contact intermittently and produced the misfire — but the scanner could not detect this because the fault was mechanical, not electronic.

4. The root cause, Don Rodrigo concluded, was not the spark plug or the coil — it was a culture issue. The younger technicians had learned to trust the scanner first and to skip the manual inspection. The English-language repair manual clearly stated, on page 78: "For recurring P0301, perform full visual inspection of the wiring harness BEFORE replacing components" — but very few technicians read that page.

5. The dealership manager decided to act in three ways. First, every service order would now include a mandatory checkbox: "Visual inspection completed: yes / no", signed by the technician. Second, Don Rodrigo would run a 4-hour weekly workshop with the younger technicians: half manual inspection technique, half English vocabulary from real service manuals. Third, the dealership invested in a digital archive of repair manuals translated and annotated in Spanish, accessible by tablet at every workstation.

6. Three months later, repeated-visit cases dropped from 9 to 1. Don Rodrigo says: "The scanner is a great tool, but it is not your brain. Your brain works only if you keep your eyes open — and if you can read what the manufacturer wrote about your car." The case is now used in INACAP training programs across the country.

Problem: 9 autos volvieron al taller 2-4 veces por la misma falla de encendido (P0301). Reclamos y reputación dañada.
Causes: (1) Dependencia excesiva del scanner. (2) Falta de inspección visual de ramal de cables. (3) Cultura de no leer manuales en inglés.
Effects: 3 garantías pagadas, trabajo gratis repetido, pérdida de confianza del cliente.
Solutions: (1) Checkbox obligatorio de inspección visual en orden de servicio. (2) Taller semanal de 4 horas (inspección + inglés técnico). (3) Archivo digital de manuales traducidos en cada estación.

📝 Actividad 3 — Comprehension & analysis (6 preguntas)

1. (Explícita) ¿Cuántos autos volvieron repetidamente al taller y por qué motivo?

Nueve autos volvieron 2, 3 o 4 veces por el mismo reclamo: fallo de encendido recurrente (código P0301).

2. (Explícita) ¿Qué pieza estaban reemplazando los técnicos antes de la investigación de Don Rodrigo?

La bujía (spark plug) y la bobina de encendido (ignition coil) del cilindro 1.

3. (Explícita) ¿Cuál era la verdadera causa raíz que encontró Don Rodrigo?

Un conector parcialmente quemado en el ramal de cables (wiring harness) cerca de la bobina del cilindro 1, que perdía contacto intermitentemente. El scanner no podía detectarlo porque la falla era mecánica, no electrónica.

4. (Inferencia) ¿Por qué Don Rodrigo dice que el problema era una "culture issue" y no un problema técnico?

Porque los técnicos habían aprendido a confiar primero en el scanner y a saltarse la inspección visual. El manual en inglés ya tenía la respuesta (página 78), pero pocos lo leían. No era falta de herramientas: era falta de método y de inglés técnico.

5. (Análisis personal) Cuando tú estás frente a un auto con falla, ¿qué haces primero: conectas el scanner o haces inspección visual? ¿Por qué?

Respuesta personal con autodiagnóstico honesto + justificación. Lo ideal es combinar ambos: inspección visual rápida → scanner → confirmación visual.

6. (Crítica) Don Rodrigo dice: "The scanner is a great tool, but it is not your brain." ¿Estás de acuerdo? Da 2 argumentos.

Respuesta personal con 2 argumentos sólidos (a favor o en contra), usando al menos un conector causa-efecto.

🗣 Actividad 4 — Solución grupal (15 min)

En grupos de 3-4, propongan una cuarta solución distinta a las que tomó la concesionaria. Construyan 3 oraciones usando conectores causa-efecto:

  1. "The root cause of the problem was __________ because __________."
  2. "As a result, we propose __________."
  3. "Therefore, the dealership should __________."

📋 Seguimiento de la clase

Formativa: el docente recoge el organizador y observa las propuestas. Criterios: (1) organizador completo, (2) propuesta con 3 oraciones y 2+ conectores, (3) uso de 4+ palabras del vocabulario, (4) participación.

🟣 Cierre (15 min)

🗣 Sharing + exit ticket

Cada grupo presenta su propuesta. El docente cierra: "A scanner reads codes. A technician reads the car. Don Rodrigo's lesson is that the scanner amplifies your brain — it does not replace it."

Preview: "Next two classes we will prepare and hold a structured debate about EVs vs. combustion vehicles."

🎫 Exit ticket

"The most important lesson from this case study is __________ because __________."
